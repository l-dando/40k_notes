## 🧭 Overview

---

Inquisitor [[Gregor Eisenhorn]] of the Ordo Xenos begins a wide‑ranging investigation into forbidden lore, hidden cult networks, and the lingering threat posed by [[Pontius Glaw]]. What starts as a hunt for heresy and xenos contamination spreads from violent frontier operations to the salons and estates of [[Gudrun]], drawing new allies such as [[Alizebeth Bequin]] and the young psyker [[Gideon Ravenor]] into Eisenhorn’s orbit.

As the inquiry deepens, Eisenhorn is forced to confront the allure of using tainted knowledge against even darker enemies. The search for the [[Necroteuch]] and the truth behind Glaw’s survival makes *Xenos* the foundation of Eisenhorn’s long moral slide, while the [[Pontius Glaw Investigation]] becomes a story of investigation, compromise, and the cost of fighting corruption from within the Imperium’s own shadows.

---

## Part One — The Shape of an Inquisitor
*Xenos* begins by establishing exactly what makes Eisenhorn compelling: he is efficient, intelligent, charismatic, and already willing to use fear and secrecy as tools. The early stages of his investigation frame the Inquisition not as a glorious shield but as a ruthless intelligence apparatus. Even before the great moral compromises begin, the novel makes clear that Eisenhorn operates in a world where legality, truth, and survival are rarely aligned.

The first movement of the story also assembles the human core of the trilogy. [[Alizebeth Bequin]] and [[Gideon Ravenor]] do not just widen Eisenhorn’s reach; they reveal the different kinds of people drawn into an inquisitor’s orbit and the cost of staying there.

---

## Part Two — Gudrun and the Glaw Web
On [[Gudrun]], the investigation becomes more layered and more intimate. Noble society, hidden corruption, and the afterlife of old aristocratic crimes turn the book from a straightforward hunt into a conspiracy narrative about influence surviving beyond death. The threat of [[Pontius Glaw]] works because it is not only physical; it is archival, social, and ideological.

This is also where Eisenhorn’s methods begin to darken in visible ways. The hunt for the [[Necroteuch]] is not simply about preventing others from using forbidden knowledge. It is also about the temptation to master that knowledge first and ask moral questions later.

---

## Part Three — Foundations of Radicalism
By the final stretch, *Xenos* has become the origin point for nearly every major tension in the Eisenhorn arc. Eisenhorn still thinks of himself as a defender of orthodoxy, but the novel shows how easily urgency becomes justification. The enemies are monstrous enough to make compromise feel reasonable, and that is precisely why the compromise matters.

The result is a 40K novel that works both as investigation and as character study. The case itself is satisfying, but its larger importance is that it begins the long conversion of Eisenhorn from righteous inquisitor into a man who increasingly believes he alone can judge which corruptions are acceptable.

## 🧠 Themes
- Investigation as a gateway to moral compromise
- Corruption surviving through wealth, secrecy, and forbidden knowledge
- The Inquisition’s methods mirroring the darkness it fights

## 📝 Notes
- [[Gregor Eisenhorn]] is most interesting when competence and self-justification start to merge.
- [[Alizebeth Bequin]] gives the trilogy one of its clearest human counterweights to inquisitorial ruthlessness.
- [[Gudrun]] helps ground the novel in Imperial society beyond battlefields and hive-war.
- The [[Necroteuch]] is important less as a relic than as a symbol of curiosity turning into dependence.

## 🔗 Connections
- Begins the main Eisenhorn arc continued in *[[Malleus]]* and *[[Hereticus]]*.
- Establishes [[Pontius Glaw]], [[Alizebeth Bequin]], and [[Gideon Ravenor]] as defining parts of Eisenhorn’s story.
- Introduces the [[Pontius Glaw Investigation]] and the lure of the [[Necroteuch]] that shape the later books.
