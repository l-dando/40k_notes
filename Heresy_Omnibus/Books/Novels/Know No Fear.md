## 🧭 Overview
*Know No Fear* captures the betrayal at [[Calth]] in brutal, escalating detail. The Ultramarines gather for a joint muster with the Word Bearers, expecting co‑operation against an ork threat, while the XVII Legion prepares a meticulously staged massacre instead.

Dan Abnett tells the disaster as a countdown to impact and then a fight for survival. The novel is a defining account of [[Roboute Guilliman|Guilliman]], the Ultramarines under sudden fratricidal attack, and the opening of the [[Battle of Calth]].

---

## Part One — Target//Acquisition
Calth is presented at the height of ordered Ultramarine confidence. Muster schedules, logistics, fleet movements, and ceremonial conjunctions all suggest control. That calm is false. The Word Bearers have already seized the fleet tender **Campanile**, seeded corruption through Calth’s systems, and placed cult elements and saboteurs where they can do the most damage.

Several viewpoints establish what is about to be broken. [[Aeonid Thiel]] waits aboard the *Macragge’s Honour* for formal censure because he has been running theoreticals about Astartes fighting Astartes. [[Remus Ventanus]] oversees the Erud Province muster and notices irregular strain in the system. [[Marius Gage]] helps manage a gathering so large that it looks like routine Imperial power. On the human side, [[Oll Persson]] gives the coming catastrophe a civilian perspective, grounded in ordinary life rather than legion command.

The Word Bearers’ side of the trap is equally clear. [[Sorot Tchure]] meets with Captain [[Honorius Luciel]], turning old comradeship into part of the deception, while [[Erebus]] and other traitor agents prepare the rituals and sabotage that will accompany open attack. Even before the first shot, strange psychic disturbances and corrupted data patterns show that the war at Calth will not be purely material.

---

## Part Two — Absolute//Overwhelm
The betrayal begins with overwhelming violence. The **Campanile** is used as a weapon against the muster in orbit, and the conjunction collapses into slaughter as Word Bearers ships open fire on vessels that had expected alliance. At almost the same moment, falling wreckage and planned sabotage devastate the surface, wrecking cities, communications, and command cohesion.

Guilliman at first struggles to reconcile what he is seeing with the impossible truth in front of him: this is not accident or panic, but a premeditated legionary ambush. Once he accepts that, he authorises the Ultramarines to return fire and the pretence of brotherhood ends. The XIII are forced, in minutes, to relearn war against former brothers.

On the surface, Ventanus and other scattered Ultramarines try to restore order while evidence of prior planning mounts around them. Muster zones become kill‑grounds. Army units and civilians are caught in the same devastation. The friendship between Luciel and Tchure, which might once have suggested reconciliation between XIII and XVII, is revealed instead as proof of how intimate the treachery really is.

---

## Part Three — System//Kill
As the fleet war rages, the battle aboard the *Macragge’s Honour* turns daemonic. The flagship is assaulted from within, and Thiel — still awaiting punishment — is forced to improvise with weapons from Guilliman’s own collection. In the fighting he saves [[Marius Gage]]’s life and proves that his supposedly shameful theoreticals were exactly the kind of thinking the Ultramarines now need.

Thiel’s central insight is simple and devastating: the Ultramarines’ greatest weakness in this moment is their own predictability, while the Word Bearers’ greatest strength is the shock of betrayal. If the XIII are to survive, they must become less rigid, more adaptive, and entirely willing to treat fellow Astartes as the enemy. What had looked like near‑treason becomes hard doctrine in the space of a single battle.

Guilliman himself is forced into impossibly direct combat during the void action. He survives a daemonic assault and exposure to hard vacuum, tears through Word Bearers in the fighting around his flagship, and becomes an emblem of Ultramarine refusal to break. The novel uses these moments to show that Guilliman is not only an administrator and strategist, but a terrifying battlefield presence.

---

## Part Four — Target//Engagement to Un//Doing
As the battle widens, Calth stops being a single engagement and becomes a theatre of fractured survival. Ventanus works among scattered survivors on the surface while the underworld beneath the planet begins to matter as much as the ruined cities above. The Word Bearers’ plan is not merely to win a fleet action, but to cripple the Ultramarines so completely that they cannot intervene elsewhere in the Heresy.

[[Kor Phaeron]] emerges as one of the chief architects of the attack in the void war. In the climactic confrontation, he attempts to use the same kind of corrupting influence that helped claim [[Horus Lupercal|Horus]], trying to break Guilliman spiritually as well as militarily. He fails. Guilliman answers by nearly killing him outright, tearing out one of Kor Phaeron’s hearts and denying the traitors the symbolic victory they want.

The Word Bearers do catastrophic damage, but they do not destroy the Ultramarines. Instead, they create a long, bitter war of attrition on and beneath Calth. By the end of the novel, the XIII are bloodied, fragmented, and fully aware of the scale of the Heresy at last. The old wound of [[Monarchia]] has become open war.

## 📝 Notes
- The countdown structure makes the ambush feel mechanical and inevitable.
- The “Mark of Calth” functions both as a date marker and as the wound this battle leaves on Ultramar.
- Thiel’s censured theoreticals become one of the novel’s clearest reversals: the idea judged improper is exactly what survival requires.
- The book is the moment the Ultramarines stop being distant from the Heresy and are forced into it.

## 🔗 Connections
- Follows directly from the ending of [[The First Heretic]], where the Word Bearers set course for [[Calth]].
- Turns the grievance born at [[Monarchia]] and the [[Censure of Monarchia]] into open legionary war.
- Reframes [[Roboute Guilliman|Guilliman]] from ordered empire‑builder into besieged war leader.
